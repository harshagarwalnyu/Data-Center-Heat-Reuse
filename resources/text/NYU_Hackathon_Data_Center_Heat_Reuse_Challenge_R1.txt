THE CHALLENGE  
Develop and defend a technically feasible, economically viable and community-centered system that captures, upgrades and delivers data-center heat to the community most suitable users. Teams that select our challenge will be expected to show how the solution provides reliable, measurable value while sharing costs, risks and responsibilities across stakeholders for two site scenarios involving an existing data center (commissioned) and a new data center (proposed).
Link to Source Documentation


TEAM DELIVERABLE 
Provide concise, evidence-based system proposal that includes the network architecture, heat source and most suitable users, required infrastructure, operating approach, and environmental and community impact. Select one setting and justify why the proposed solution is appropriate for that local context. Demonstrate how heat supply and demand are matched by temperature, capacity, timing, seasonality and continuity; explain how cooling reliability and continuity of heat supply are protected; quantify measurable value for the data center, heat users and community; and show how costs, risks, ownership and responsibilities are shared across stakeholders.

Regenerative Design Data Centers
SITE 1  |  URBAN

111 8th Avenue Data Center (Commissioned)

Location: 111 8th Avenue, New York, NY 10011

Operator: Multi-tenant carrier hotel and data center facility

Scenario
An urban, multi-tenant data center located near diverse potential heat users and existing building-energy infrastructure.

Design question
How can the data center become a reliable thermal energy hub for the most suitable local heat off takers?

Consider
• Identify and justify the most suitable heat off takers
• Match supply and demand by temperature, capacity, timing, seasonality and continuity
• Heat upgrading, distribution and building interfaces
• Backup heat and continuity without compromising data-center cooling reliability
• Costs, risks, ownership and responsibilities across stakeholders | SITE 2  |  SUBURBAN / RESIDENTIAL

Lake Hawkeye Data Center (Proposed)

Location: Former Cayuga Power Plant Site, Lansing, NY

Developer/Operator: TeraWulf

Scenario
A proposed data center at a former power-plant site near residential and community uses with potential for local heat-reuse infrastructure.

Design question
How can recovered heat reliably serve the most suitable nearby residential and community heat users?

Consider
• Identify and justify the most suitable heat off takers
• Match supply and demand by temperature, capacity, timing, seasonality and continuity
• Domestic hot water, building heating and community facilities
• Heat-pump needs, distribution and backup heat
• Affordability, community acceptance and continuity of heat supply
