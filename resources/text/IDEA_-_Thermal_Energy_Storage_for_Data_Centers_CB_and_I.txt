--- page 5 ---
Data Center Concerns and Opportunities
These are “Mission Critical Facilities”
• Require redundancy, reliability, and resilience,
• High capital and operating costs for cooling, and
• Heat rejection may not match variable demand.
Energy Storage can help address all those issues:
• Cool TES provides emergency back-up.
• Cool TES can also lower peak demand and cost.
• Hot TES can balance heat rejection with demand.


--- page 6 ---
Wind Under-Performs at Peak Demand
Typical ERCOT Data:
• During peak grid demand, wind only
produces ~20% of its nameplate
capacity (and too much on some nights).
Energy trading value varies enormously,
due to high on-peak demand and excess night wind:
• In Texas – as high as $9.00/kWh on peak days; but
as low as negative $0.10/kWh on some nights
• In Nebraska – as low as negative $0.20/kWh on some nights


--- page 7 ---
ERCOT’s Peak Demand Wind Issue
• When hitting a peak demand of:
~70,000 MW
• Installed rated wind generation was:
~23,000 MW
• But actual wind output during that peak: <600 MW
 - Wind output was only ~2.5% of its rated capacity!
As a Result:
1.
All this expensive, subsidized wind generation did not reduce the need for 
conventional generation.
2.
Only Energy Storage can make fuller use of variable renewable power 
investments.

Energy Storage – Technology Options
• Pumped Hydro-electric storage (PH)
• Compressed Air Energy Storage (CAES)
• Mechanical Flywheel Storage
• Traditional Batteries (Lead-Acid and Sodium Sulfur)
• Advanced Batteries (Lithium-Ion is most common)

• Thermal Energy Storage (TES)

• Cool TES (Ice TES, Chilled Water, Low Temp Fluid)

Thermal Energy Storage (TES)
• Most is Cool TES, but there can also be Hot TES.
• TES de-couples the generation of thermal capacity from the demand for 
thermal capacity.
• The TES medium is “charged” (cooled, frozen, or heated) during off-peak 
periods of low demand and thus low cost or low value energy.
• TES is “discharged” during on-peak periods of high demand and thus high cost 
or high value energy.
Unlike other Energy Storage options, TES also provides thermal capacity to meet 
peak loads, often reducing net capital cost by reducing required chiller or boiler 
capacity.


--- page 12 ---
TES Application for Data Centers
• Small-volume, high-flow CHW TES for back-up cooling during an 
emergency (chiller recovery following power loss)
• Large-volume CHW TES or Low Temp Fluid TES for both emergency back-
up and shifting electric energy use & demand from high cost on-peak to 
lower cost off-peak periods (as well as right-sizing chiller plant capacity 
for a potential net CapEx saving)
• CHW TES for increased use of seasonal “free cooling” systems
• Cool TES to balance & increase use of variable wind & solar power
• Cool TES to enhance Turbine Inlet Cooling of on-site Gas Turbines
• HW TES to balance heat rejection with demand for heat
And always, enhancing redundancy, reliability, and resilience

Case Study – Data Center CHW TES 
State Farm Insurance – Bloomingtion, Illinois
Corporate data processing campus CHW TES:
•
Phase 1 (1994) - 1st 44,800 Ton-hr tank (4 million gals)
•
Phase 2 (2000) - 2nd 44,800 Ton-hr tank (4 million gals)
TES not just for back-up; it lowers elec demand/energy costs:
•
Added N+2 redundancy to meet peak load.
•
Reduced capital cost vs non-TES chiller capacity.
•
Shifts 13,000 Tons of peak cooling and ~10 MW peak load.
Modeling showed TES had 3% reduction in annual kWh/Ton-hr.

Summary and Conclusions
• TES is often superior to batteries or other Energy Storage options.
• CHW TES can often address specific Data Center challenges:
• Back-up cooling during power emergencies
• Reduce on-peak power demand and operating cooling costs,
• Often meet peak cooling loads at reduced net capital cost,
• Maximize use of intermittent wind & solar power,
• Maximize on-site gas turbine output in hot weather,
• HW TES  to balance heat rejection with demand for heat, and
• All TES to enhance redundancy, reliability, and resilience.
• Thermal Energy Storage, particularly CHW TES, has:
• Numerous Data Center examples, with many repeat owners,
• Low unit capital cost ($/ton-hr and $/kWh) in large sizes.
Explore TES early in concept designs to maximize value.
