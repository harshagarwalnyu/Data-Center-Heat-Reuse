BACKGROUND & PURPOSE
The campus of First Presbyterian Church in Jamaica, Queens is adjacent to a large Verizon 
switchgear and data facility that continuously releases waste heat. With support from 
NYSERDA, urbs  has evaluated the technical feasibility of capturing this heat and reusing it to 
provide space heating for the church’s community center – the 100-year-old Magill Building. 
This workshop brought church leaders together with representatives from Verizon,  
equipment manufacturers, engineering firms, and public agencies to review the feasibility 
findings, discuss technical and institutional pathways, and identify next steps for both the 
FPCJ demonstration project and broader scaling opportunities.
3:00 PM – Welcome, Introductions & Site Tour
Participants gathered in the Magill Building at the FPCJ campus.
Rev. Patrick O’Connor welcomed the attendees and framed the significance of the project 
for the congregation and the surrounding community.
Joe Morris presented the agenda, followed by a short round of introductions around the 
table.
FPCJ Property Manager Raffique Ali led everyone for a short site tour to show the dry coolers 
currently in use by Verizon to reject heat from the rear of their facility, just beyond FPCJ’s 
property line.

3:15 PM – Background & Goals
Joe Morris (Metro IAF) outlined two key energy-related challenges facing FPCJ: high energy 
costs and chronic performance problems with the current VRF system, including refrigerant 
leaks. Joe highlighted the pioneering leadership of the church in deciding to install a heat-
pump system and to disconnect from gas.
Michael Reed (NYSERDA) described NYSERDA’s support for FPCJ’s heat pump project, and 
the agency’s commitment to continue to work with FPCJ to achieve positive 
outcomes. Michael noted that the opportunity to capture and reuse waste heat at FPCJ is 
aligned with NYSERDA’s goals and a growing interest in heat recovery across the state.
3:30 PM – Heat Recovery Feasibility at FPCJ
urbs. Feasibility Findings
Will Sibia and Ellinor Granström (urbs.) presented findings from the heat recovery feasibility 
study and conceptual design, including an assessment of available waste heat from the 
adjacent Verizon facility, the proposed system configuration, and preliminary energy 
projections.
Key finding: A small fraction of the heat currently rejected from the Verizon facility could be 
captured, with proven technology, and used to provide most of the heating needs of the 
Magill Building – significantly reducing FPCJ’s heating costs and grid demands with no 
additional operating costs to Verizon.
4


--- page 5 ---
3:30 PM – Heat Recovery Feasibility at FPCJ
Technology & Case Studies – Danfoss & Alfa Laval
Emma Bolus (Alfa Laval) and Bruce Parsons (Danfoss) provided technical commentary on 
heat pump and heat exchanger systems suitable for this project. Both companies noted that 
waste heat recovery is increasingly recognized as one of the world’s largest untapped energy 
resources, and that existing and new data centers can employ these approaches to share 
excess heat with neighboring buildings and communities. Key takeaways: Proven technical 
solutions are already widely used, and EU regulators have started mandating that data 
centers share the energy they reject with neighboring buildings or thermal networks.
Execution and Deployment in New York – Endurant
Tony Amis expressed Endurant’s keen interest in supporting the design and execution of 
projects like this one, and offered that he saw no technical barriers to implementing this 
concept at FPCJ.
Verizon’s Response
Rev. O’Connor asked for Verizon’s perspective. Verizon’s John Barstow noted the 
company’s interest in executing model heat recovery projects like this one, but shared some 
of the challenges this project would face. He stressed that the Verizon facility is not a 
traditional data center, but rather a switchgear facility housing multi-generational 
equipment in multiple rooms that are being cooled individually and reject heat via individual 
dry coolers rather than a central energy rejection loop. He noted that the long-term plan for 
the facility and its equipment is not fully understood at this moment, and suggested that 
redundancy measures would be critical to ensure the proposed heating system at FPCJ 
would not solely rely on Verizon’s waste heat in case things changed. This recommendation 
is in line with urbs’s recommendations to the church.
5


--- page 6 ---
4:00 PM - Q&A and Discussion
Open discussion followed, covering regulatory considerations, financing, and various 
system configurations. NYC EDC expressed a willingness to work with the parties to navigate 
regulatory issues involved in transferring heat from one property to another. NYSERDA also 
expressed an interest in learning more about potential barriers to the project and working 
with the parties to overcome those barriers.
4:10 PM – Scaling Heat Recovery
The group discussed the larger significance of this project – i.e., how lessons from the FPCJ 
project could inform broader implementation at data centers across New York State and 
beyond. Danfoss and Alfa Laval agreed to serve as technical resources for the fact-finding 
needed to advance this project as well as a broader national discussion on how data centers 
can be responsible neighbors and energy consumers.
6
As a for-profit enterprise, Verizon would also have to understand the cost-benefit 
implications of the project. Despite the obstacles, he expressed Verizon’s commitment to 
explore this further.

4.20 PM – Conclusion and Note of Thanks
Rev. O’Connor reiterated how the church and Magill Building have served Jamaica over many 
generations, and how this plan would allow the church to again demonstrate a solution for

dry cooler glycol loops and transfers it through an intermediary 
energy transfer station to a heat pump system serving the 
church campus. The system returns cooler glycol back to 
Verizon’s infrastructure while repurposing heat that would 
otherwise be rejected into the atmosphere. The current concept 
envisions retaining existing VRF systems for cooling while heat 
pumps provide low-temperature heating through an ambient 
loop distribution system.

FULL HEATING DEMAND CAN BE MET
Preliminary analysis indicates that the church's entire annual

with Verizon and the extent of dry cooler integration.

AI and data centers are among the fastest-growing sources of 
electricity demand in New York State. Most of that energy is 
consumed by cooling systems operating continuously, even during 
winter. All electricity entering a data center ultimately becomes heat—
typically wasted despite being a stable and reusable energy resource. 
The campus of First Presbyterian Church in Jamaica is adjacent to a 
Verizon switchgear and data facility releasing continuous waste heat. 
With support from NYSERDA, Urban Systems (URBS), Danfoss, and 
Alfa Laval are advancing a conceptual design to capture this heat and 
reuse it to provide space heating for the church’s community center.
Waste heat recovery is increasingly recognized as one of the world’s 
largest untapped energy resources, with industry leaders such as 
Danfoss and Alfa Laval helping advance systems that improve 
efficiency and reduce grid strain, operating costs, and emissions. 
Existing as well as new data centers can employ these approaches to 
share excess heat with neighboring buildings and communities.
This visit initiates dialogue around public policy that supports waste 
heat capture and establishes performance expectations for large, 
cooling-dominant facilities like data centers—reducing system-wide 
energy waste and emissions.  This is one of many initiatives that will 
be needed to ensure that AI and data centers can responsibly co-exist 
with the rest of us.
