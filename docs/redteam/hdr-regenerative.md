# HDR regenerative design red-team — Thermal Commons (Lake Hawkeye / Lansing, NY)

Role: HDR regenerative design lead, scoring the proposal the way the site-pack framework scores a project. Team: Thermal Commons co-op (Harsh Agarwal, Linson Lee, Aryaman Bhaskar, Philip Matchev). Site under review: Site 2, Lake Hawkeye / former Cayuga plant, Lansing, NY (TeraWulf). Comparison site: Site 1, 111 8th Ave, New York City.

This file judges the proposal. It does not replace `docs/proposal/13-regenerative-scorecard.md`. Where the live app and the audit disagree, the sentence a judge will hear is the one in `web/public/data/site2.json` `hdr_scorecard`, not the corrected figure.

Scale used here: 1 missing, 2 partial, 3 adequate for this pitch, 4 strong (a cited local number that survives questions), 5 regenerative. A 5 means the pack’s own test is met: net-positive renewal of a social or ecological function, measured, and written so the town could accept the project. “Less harm than a bare data center” caps a domain at 3.

## How HDR will read this

The pack defines regenerative design as design that “emulates natural systems for the continuous renewal of social and ecological functions” (`resources/text/2026_10_01_Hackathon_NYU_-_Suburban_Site_-_Lake_Hawkeye.txt` p. 2) (verified 2026-10-03). The spectrum is three names — Community, Ecology, Health — from degenerative (net harmful) to regenerative (net positive) (same file, p. 4) (verified 2026-10-03). The seven domains and the action list sit on p. 5: eliminate greenhouse-gas emissions; sequester more carbon in materials than the rest of the materials emit; serve the community better; give equity of access to the design process; re-establish ecosystem biodiversity; improve human health; connect people to nature in buildings; avoid harm to other species; conserve community water; keep occupant water quality; manage stormwater without harming other species; avoid soil contamination; species at risk (the extract’s last bullet is the phrase “IMPACTING SPECIES AT RISK”) (verified 2026-10-03). Page 6 adds the service lines HDR actually sells, including post-occupancy evaluation and measurement (verified 2026-10-03). The HDR resource deck has ERE, ERF, and a water-use spectrum. It does not contain this KPI list (`resources/text/NYU_BAC_Hackathon_HDR_Waste_Heat_Reuse_2026.1002.txt` pp. 17–18 and p. 55; `research/digest-organizer.md` §1.1) (verified 2026-10-03).

Place, not the data center, is what the pack measured. At Lake Hawkeye: no designated disadvantaged communities nearby (p. 24); minority 24th, poverty 28th, age 65+ 56th (pp. 37–39); mental health 27th (p. 14); asthma 51st, heart disease 42nd, stroke 34th (pp. 33–35); cancer “very high just across the lake,” communities in the 90th percentile, which the pack ties to an older population (pp. 36, 39); air “Good” on 0% of days in both the 10-year and 5-year windows (pp. 28–29); ozone 20th, PM2.5 4th (pp. 31–32); a statewide line of over 1,900 deaths and $5.6 billion that is not a site total (p. 30); noise 41.9 dB versus 40.6 dB at Taughannock Falls State Park (pp. 10, 13); agriculture the greatest biodiversity threat, cumulative threat “not much” (pp. 11–12); water stress, drought, and groundwater decline all low–medium (pp. 15–17); direct discharge to water 44th percentile, and runoff from the site does reach Cayuga Lake (p. 18); lake and an inlet impaired for phosphorus (p. 19); FEMA flood layer incomplete (p. 20); precipitation +3 inches within 10 years (p. 21); tornado risk 1st percentile nationally (p. 22); social vulnerability described as able to avoid or bounce back (p. 23); about +3 °F by 2050 and up to 69 days above 90 °F versus 19 today, and about +12 °F by 2080 (pp. 26–27) (verified 2026-10-03). Page 9 (“Project Context + Attributes”) and page 25 (“Carbon Lifecycle”) are titles only in the text extract. Acreage, FTE, and lifecycle tonnes sometimes quoted from those slides are [unverified] in this file. Do not say them.

The proposal’s own screen (`site2.json` `hdr_scorecard`, generated 2026-10-04) asks HDR to accept seven claims: $735/yr per home; 52,841 MWh/yr fossil displaced; 11,455 t CO2/yr; “5,500 t/yr fish + produce” as the nutrients metric; 0 gal/yr and 970 MWh/yr fan energy; “10 ha greenhouse on former coal site”; “500 homes” as the air metric. `docs/audit/numbers-impact.md` already marks the carbon, fossil, jobs, and food figures wrong or assumptive. An HDR reviewer who opens the Impact screen will score the printed claim, then deduct for the correction.

Verdict in one line: this is the right place, and it is not yet a regenerative project. It is a binding harm-limit and a heat-affordability condition. Say that. A claim that all seven domains are regenerative will fail p. 4.

Site choice still holds on the pack’s own contrast. At 111 8th Ave the pack records 56 dB versus a 43.9 dB baseline (urban pack p. 13), social vulnerability in the 79th percentile (p. 23), a cogen plant “right next to a disadvantaged community” at 654 lb CO2/MWh (p. 24), Hudson River impairment (p. 19), and 5% / 10% of days worse than “Good,” described as getting worse (pp. 28–29). The ozone slide suspects diesel backup generators on the block to the west (83rd percentile ozone; p. 31). The PM2.5 slide’s extract text says “52nd percentile for Ozone” and 92nd on the block west (p. 32) — quote that wording; do not silently relabel it (`resources/text/2026_10_01_Hackathon_NYU_-_Urban_Site_-_111_8th_Ave.txt`) (verified 2026-10-03). Lake Hawkeye’s cleaner baseline is why the project was picked. It also raises the bar: there is less damage to “fix,” so noise, phosphorus, and stormwater have to stay clean.

## Domain notes

What the proposal can say: displaced combustion. This lane did not recompute that audit. Indoor-air or hospital outcomes are [unverified].

The climate slide HDR will hold up is heat, not heating fuel: about +3 °F by 2050 and up to 69 days above 90 °F versus 19 today (pack p. 26) (verified 2026-10-03). ### Air

Score 2. The pack’s outdoor air is already the outcome a regenerative project would hope to protect: 0% of days worse than “Good,” ozone 20th, PM2.5 4th (pp. 28–29, 31–32) (verified 2026-10-03). The proposal does not show tons of PM2.5 or NOx. The Air petal metric is “500 homes” (`site2.json` `hdr_scorecard`). Those 500 are a corridor signup scenario (`impact.homes_served`), not 500 homes leaving combustion. The audit’s fuel split implies on the order of 275 of them (`docs/proposal/13-regenerative-scorecard.md`, citing Census B25040). Treat “500 homes leave combustion” as a misread.

Backup is a new combustion source: 373 MWh/yr, 0.73% of delivered heat, and unmet hours are 0 only because boilers are sized to 100% of peak (`site2.json` `totals`). Say that in the same breath as “fewer combustion appliances.”

Noise is on the biodiversity slides, and a health reviewer will ask it here anyway. The site is 41.9 dB against 40.6 dB at the state park (pack pp. 10, 13) (verified 2026-10-03). TeraWulf’s rejection path is air-cooled dry coolers (https://lakehawkeyedata.com/closed-loop-cooling , row 4a) (verified 2026-10-03). This proposal keeps those coolers. A decibel change from fewer fan hours is [unverified]. The Lake Hawkeye air slides do not name diesel generators. A generator count is [unverified]. Do not import the New York City pack’s diesel sentence (urban pack p. 31).

Fix: “Outdoor air is already Good. We protect it by not adding a cooling tower, and we disclose 373 MWh/yr of backup fuel. The air benefit we will defend is displaced propane and oil, on the order of the audited 52,016 MWh/yr, not 500 homes and not a particle inventory.”

### Carbon

Score 3. This is the only ecology domain with a method HDR already uses.

ERF = reuse energy / IT energy (`NYU_BAC_Hackathon_HDR_Waste_Heat_Reuse_2026.1002.txt` p. 18) (verified 2026-10-03). The model prints **0.0462** (`site2.json` `impact.erf`). The audit says do not “fix” it (`docs/audit/numbers-impact.md`). It is small because customers are small: delivered heat is 50,576 MWh against 777.6 GWh available, 6.5% (`site2.json` `supply` and `totals`). At 320 MW critical IT the same customers would make ERF worse; the published full-build case does not add heat users (`docs/proposal/13-regenerative-scorecard.md` on `extras.scenarios.dc_320MW_full_build`). TeraWulf’s 5 August 2026 release is about 400 MW gross / 320 MW critical IT, operations about 2029, with no phase split in that filing (https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1083301/000108330126000162/a_wulfearningsreleaseq22026.htm , row 3a) (verified 2026-10-03). The 150 MW IT case is the model base. Do not print 4.6% next to a 400 MW story.

ERE prints 1.154 from an ASSUMPTION PUE of 1.2 (`docs/audit/numbers-impact.md`). The deck’s rule is ERE ≥ 1.0 and ERE ≤ PUE (HDR deck p. 17) (verified 2026-10-03). Say the PUE is assumed. The deck’s worked example (IT 1,000 kWh, facility 1,300 kWh, 200 kWh reused, ERE 1.1, p. 18) (verified 2026-10-03) is a 20% reuse share. Ours is 4.6%.

Operational carbon: the app tile is 11,456 t/yr (`impact.co2_avoided_t_yr`) and the petal says 11,455. The audit says publish **11,408 t CO2/yr**. Grid factor used is eGRID2023 NYUP 242.8 lb CO2e/MWh (https://www.epa.gov/egrid , row 10a) (verified 2026-10-03). Cars: the app says 2,490. The audit’s equivalencies path is 2,659 at 4.29 t/vehicle-year, or 2,480 if the team keeps 4.6 t on purpose (`docs/audit/numbers-impact.md`). Marginal-grid 0.315 kg/kWh and the “10,717 t” string are [unverified] as a factor. Do not lead with them.

Page 5’s first two carbon tests are failed on purpose if we are honest. The proposal does not eliminate greenhouse-gas emissions. It displaces a slice of propane and oil and then subtracts heat-pump electricity. Sequestering more carbon in materials than the rest of the materials emit is absent. Pack p. 25 is an image in the extract; any tonne figure read off that chart is [unverified]. Do not invent a materials sink to fill the slide.

Fix: one carbon sentence — “11,408 tonnes a year on the upstate grid, and an energy-reuse factor of 4.6% because we only sell the heat we can sign. We are not net zero, and we have no materials balance.”

HDR’s own water spectrum lists “Closed-Loop Zero Evaporation” and “Data Center Heat Export” as separate zero-water items (HDR deck p. 55) (verified 2026-10-03). Do not merge them into one lake-saved claim. Industry-average site WUE cited in the digest (1.8 L/kWh, HDR deck p. 15) is not a Lake Hawkeye measurement [unverified] for this design.

The pack’s water work that is still blank: direct discharge 44th percentile and runoff reaches the lake (p. 18); phosphorus-impaired lake (p. 19); FEMA maps that “drop off” (p. 20); precipitation +3 inches within 10 years (p. 21) (verified 2026-10-03). ### Nutrients

Score 2. This is the domain HDR’s place data hands the team, and the petal spends it on the wrong unit.

Cayuga Lake and an inlet downstream are impaired for phosphorus (pack p. 19) (verified 2026-10-03). The scorecard cites a DEC release that an EPA-approved phosphorus TMDL recommends a 30% watershed reduction, and that nonpoint sources are the bulk of the load (https://dec.ny.gov/news/press-releases/2024/9/dec-announces-epa-approved-pollution-prevention-plan-for-cayuga-lake-watershed , used in `docs/proposal/13-regenerative-scorecard.md`) (verified 2026-10-03). The design intent that matches those facts is no process discharge from the fish system or the fertilizer loop to the lake or to a ditch that reaches it. That is “do not add phosphorus.” It is not a TMDL credit unless DEC says so. Kilograms of phosphorus or nitrogen kept out of the lake are [unverified].

The petal metric “5500 t/yr fish + produce” is yield. The audit splits it into 4,000 t produce at an assumed 40 kg/m² plus 1,500 t fish, both ASSUMPTION, and says RII does not publish 40 kg/m² (`docs/audit/numbers-impact.md`). Food tonnes are not nutrient tonnes.

Soil contamination is the paired p. 5 test. Food grown on a former coal plant without a contamination screen can fail nutrients and human health together. Contamination status of the pad is [unverified]. Do not claim the produce is clean.

Fix, spoken: “The lake is impaired for phosphorus. The growing loop does not discharge to it. We are not claiming a tonne of phosphorus removed, and we are not claiming the food tonnes are that removal. Food does not go on the pad until the soil is screened.”

## Lens rollup

**Community (3).** Net harmful would be a data center the town is already moving to draft a ban against, with no heat and no agreement. The proposal moves that to “conditional.” It does not reach net positive community renewal: the corridor that carries the household story fails the plan’s own density screen and needs $26.01 million; the town main fails outright; jobs and food on the Impact screen are not defendable; design-process equity is unnamed in the scorecard JSON.

**Ecology (2).** Net harmful would be evaporative cooling plus a fertilized discharge into a phosphorus lake, on new agricultural land, against a 41.9 dB baseline. The proposal avoids that picture and then stops. Avoidance is not renewal. Carbon is a real but small operational displacement (ERF 4.6%). Water claimed is zero gallons. Biodiversity and nutrients are intentions.

**Health (2).** The pack’s human-health percentiles are ordinary, the air is already Good, and the future-climate stress is heat. The proposal’s health offer is winter combustion displacement. That is worth saying. It is not “create conditions that improve human health” at the level p. 5 writes it, and it does not answer 69 days above 90 °F.

## What not to say to HDR

- That the proposal is regenerative across Community, Ecology, and Health. Ecology and Health are avoidance.
