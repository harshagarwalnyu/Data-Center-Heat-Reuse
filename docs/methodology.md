# Methodology

This document describes the 8,760-hour pipeline that produces every number in the Thermal Commons app. It follows the code in `src/heatreuse/`, one step per module. Inputs live in `config/*.yaml` and are listed with sources in [assumptions.md](assumptions.md). Outputs are in [results.md](results.md). The JSON contract that carries them to the web app is in [architecture.md](architecture.md).

Numbers quoted here are the base case in `outputs/site2.json` unless stated. They are regenerated by `uv run python -m heatreuse.run`, so treat the file as the source of truth if a figure here drifts.

## Pipeline at a glance

```
Weather (TMYx, 8,760 h)
   |
   +--> Supply   (IT load x capture fraction x availability)         supply.py
   +--> Demand   (3 rings: on-site, corridor, town)                  demand.py + heatpump.py
   |
   v
Dispatch (direct HX -> source-side tank -> backup boilers)           dispatch.py
   |
   +--> Finance  (capex by asset life, CRF, LCOH, tariff, NPV)       finance.py
   +--> Impact   (CO2, ERF/ERE, jobs, food)                          impact.py
   |
   v
Report -> outputs/site2.json, site1.json, offtakers.json             report.py, scoring.py, run.py
```

Orchestration is `model.py:simulate` (weather, supply, demand, dispatch) and `model.py:full` (adds finance and impact). Every hourly array has length 8,760. A test asserts this (`tests/test_model.py::test_8760_rows`).

## 1. Weather

`weather.py:load_temps` returns the hourly dry-bulb temperature (deg C) for the site.

- Source: Climate.OneBuilding TMYx 2009-2023 EPW, Ithaca Tompkins Regional Airport, WMO 725155. Site 1 uses the Central Park file, WMO 725053.
- `_read_epw` reads column 7 (dry bulb) after the 8 header lines. If a leap-year file has 8,784 rows, Feb 29 is dropped so every site has exactly 8,760 hours.
- Fallback order: raw EPW in `data/raw/` (git-ignored because of size), then the cached CSV `data/processed/weather_<site>.csv` (committed), then a labelled synthetic sinusoid. The provenance string is written to `meta.weather` in the JSON. A test checks that the temperature range and HDD are plausible for Ithaca (`test_weather_is_real_and_plausible`), which a synthetic series would not pass.
- Heating degree days for the Ithaca file: HDD65F = 6,646 (`research/model-notes.md`, computed by `weather.py:hdd_f`). This is below the long-term normal of about 7,000, so modeled heating loads are slightly low.

## 2. Supply: heat available at the capture point

`supply.py:heat_available_mw` (MW thermal, hourly):

```
IT_load(h)  = min( IT_MW x load_factor x (1 + diurnal_amp x sin(2 pi (hod - 14)/24)),  IT_MW )
A(h)        = IT_load(h) x capture_fraction x availability(h)
```

| Term | Base value | Config key |
|---|---|---|
| IT_MW | 150 MW (phase 1; 320 MW critical IT is the full-build scenario) | `supply.it_load_mw` |
| load_factor | 0.80 | `supply.load_factor` |
| diurnal_amp | 0.03 | `supply.diurnal_amp` |
| capture_fraction | 0.75 (scenarios 0.40 and 0.85) | `supply.capture_fraction` |
| capture_temp | 50 deg C direct liquid cooling return | `supply.capture_temp_c` |

`availability(h)` is `supply.py:availability_mask`: a seeded (`outage_seed: 7`) set of outages with exponential durations (mean 22 h, minimum 2 h) until 1 percent of hours are lost, about 88 h per year. It represents sidestream trips and maintenance, not data-center downtime.

Design rule carried through the whole model: the heat exchanger is a **sidestream** off the cooling loop and the **dry coolers stay the primary rejection path**. Heat reuse never sets a data-center setpoint. When the heat side stops, the stream falls back to the dry coolers (see [cooling-integration.md](cooling-integration.md) and the "cooling-always-wins" clause in [ownership-deal.md](ownership-deal.md)).

Base result (`outputs/site2.json`, `supply`): 777.6 GWh/yr available, 88.8 MW average. The reuse system delivers 6.5 percent of it, so supply is never the binding constraint. That is a finding, not an input: see the flat "Data-center IT load" bar in the tornado ([results.md](results.md)).

## 3. Demand: three rings

`demand.py:build` returns one dict per ring with hourly arrays: `D` customer heat (MW), `L` pipe loss (MW), `E` heat-pump electricity (MW), `S` draw from the data-center side (MW, equal to `D + L - E` for heat-pump rings).

### Ring 1, on-site campus (`demand.py:onsite`)

Direct heat exchange at 45 deg C (50 deg C capture minus 2 x 3 K approach). No heat pump.

- Greenhouse: `D = U_eff x area x max(0, 18.5 - T_out) / 1e6`, with U_eff 3.5 W/m2K, 10 ha, setpoint 18.5 deg C. Checked against the Rural Innovation Initiative benchmark of about 1 MWth per hectare, 2 acres per MW (RII p7, p34). The model peak is higher than that benchmark; `extras.greenhouse_check` in the JSON reports both.
- Aquaculture (RAS): 1,500 t/yr x 3.0 kWh/kg. 70 percent is constant, 30 percent follows heating degree hours below 15 deg C.
- Recreation and pool: 600 MWh/yr pool plus a 4,000 m2 building at 140 kWh/m2, 15 percent DHW (flat), the rest weather-driven.
- Pipe loss: 0.5 km x 15 W/m, constant.

### Ring 2, corridor homes on an ambient loop (`demand.py:corridor`)

An uninsulated 18 deg C "fifth-generation" loop with a heat pump in each home.

- Customers: 500 signed homes, uptake 0.70, so the pipe is sized for 500 / 0.70 = 714 potential homes. Pipe length = 3.0 km trunk + 714 x 25 m frontage = 20.9 km.
- Annual demand per home 27 MWh: 23.5 space, 3.5 DHW.
- Space heat follows heating degree hours below 15.5 deg C. DHW follows a fixed diurnal shape.
- Sink temperature uses a heating curve (`heatpump.py:weather_comp`): `T_sink = clip(35 + 0.6 x (15 - T_out), 35, 55)` deg C. DHW is 55 deg C.
- Loop loss 6 W/m over the full pipe length.
- Linear heat density = 13,500 MWh/yr / 20.9 km = 0.65 MWh per metre per year, below the 1.5 gate used in PLAN.md and the risk register ([risk-matrix.md](risk-matrix.md), R09).

### Ring 3, town center (`demand.py:town`)

School campus, town hall, library and senior apartments on a 55-65 deg C hot loop, 14 km of pipe (11 km trunk plus 3 km spur). Weather-compensated sink `clip(55 + 0.5 x (10 - T_out), 55, 65)`. If `capture_temp - 2 x approach >= sink` the stream feeds the loop directly with no lift (`direct` mask). Ring 3 is **excluded from base totals** (`town.include_in_base: false`) and reported separately as a gated, conditional phase.

## 4. Heat pump COP

`heatpump.py:cop`:

```
COP = clip( eta x T_sink_K / ( T_sink_K - T_source_K + 2 x approach_K ),  2.0,  6.0 )
```

- `eta = 0.5` of the Carnot limit (config `hp.eta`).
- `approach_K = 3` per heat exchanger, so the lift is widened by 6 K.
- Clip range [2, 6] follows the organizer ranges (COP 2 to 6, `research/digest-organizer.md` section 2.3; COP 2 to 5 typical in the Topic 5 deck).
- Temperatures are converted to Kelvin inside the function (`+ 273.15`).

Worked examples, computed from the formula: corridor space heat at a 55 deg C sink and 18 deg C source gives 0.5 x 328.15 / (55 - 18 + 6) = 3.82. At a 35 deg C sink the unclipped value is 6.7 and the clip returns 6.0. The seasonal average over the corridor is reported as `totals.avg_cop` (4.71 in the base case).

The clip at 6.0 is generous at the top end. `research/model-notes.md` flags the central heat pump at the cap as optimistic; it matters only for the town ring, which is outside base totals.

Incumbent comparator, an air-source heat pump (`finance.py:incumbents`): same formula with `eta = 0.4`, sink 40 deg C, approach 5 K, source = hourly outdoor temperature, clip [1.3, 6]. The seasonal COP is `sum(D) / sum(D / COP_hour)` and is 2.53 in the base case.

## 5. Storage and dispatch

`dispatch.py:dispatch` runs a sequential 8,760-step loop over the active rings. Let `S(h)` be the combined data-center-side draw and `A(h)` the available heat.

Storage sizing: `capacity = 6 h x max(S)` MWh thermal, a source-side hot-water tank on a 20 K temperature difference (50/30 deg C). Volume `= capacity x 1000 / (1.163 x 20)` m3, where 1.163 kWh per m3 per K is the specific heat of water. Base result: 5,558 m3 (about 129 MWh). The tank starts full, loses 1 percent per day, and charges at no more than `capacity / 4 h`.

Each hour, in this order:

1. Standing loss is deducted from the state of charge.
2. **Direct**: `d = min(A, S)` flows from the data center to the network.
3. **Discharge**: any shortfall `S - d` is taken from the tank up to its state of charge.
4. `f = (d + discharge) / S` is the fraction of demand served from data-center heat. The remainder is covered by backup boilers: `backup = (1 - f) x (D + L)`.
5. **Charge**: surplus `A - d` charges the tank, limited by charge rate and headroom.

Backup boilers are sized at 100 percent of peak (`backup.capacity_share: 1.0`), so `unmet_hours = 0` by construction. The honest resilience metrics are `totals.peak_share_dc_pct` and `totals.backup_share_annual_pct` (95.3 percent of peak-hour heat from the data center, 0.73 percent of annual heat from backup in the base case). A test shrinks backup to prove unmet hours can become non-zero (`test_unmet_is_zero_by_construction_only_when_backup_full`).

The tank protects against capture outages, not against demand peaks: supply is about 15 times demand. Customers also keep their existing propane or oil systems for the first years (see the heat-continuity cascade in [risk-matrix.md](risk-matrix.md)).

Energy balance is checked hourly and annually: `dispatch.py:balance_residual` must be below 0.1 percent (`test_energy_balance_closes`, `test_hourly_balance`), and the tank state of charge must stay within [0, capacity] (`test_soc_nonnegative_and_bounded`).

## 6. Finance

All dollars are 2026 USD. Unit costs are assumptions tagged in `config/finance.yaml` and in the capex line `source` fields.

### Capex (`finance.py:capex_lines`)

Eleven lines for phases 1 and 2: data-center-side sidestream interface (`$120/kW` of peak draw), tank (`$300/m3`), on-site pipe (`$900/m`), customer substations (`$100/kW`), corridor loop pipe (`$450/m`), building heat pumps (`$16,000` each), service laterals (`$2,500` per home), pumps and plant (`$40/kW`), backup boilers (`$120/kW`), soft costs (15 percent) and contingency (20 percent). Town-ring lines are added only when the town ring is active.

### Annualization by asset life (`finance.py:crf`, `allocate`)

```
CRF(r, n) = r (1 + r)^n / ((1 + r)^n - 1)
```

Each capex line is assigned a life class by `life_class`: pipe and laterals 30 years, tank 30 years, equipment (heat pumps, heat exchangers, boilers, pumps) 20 years. Shared lines are split across rings by delivered-plus-loss energy, and soft cost and contingency are spread pro rata. At 7 percent, CRF(7%, 30) = 0.0806 and CRF(7%, 20) = 0.0944 (computed from the formula). Using two lives captures heat-pump replacement without a separate replacement schedule.

### Opex (`finance.py:evaluate`)

- Fixed: 1.5 percent of ring capex per year, plus for the corridor $150 per home heat-pump service and $120 per home customer cost, plus a $400,000 per year operator and insurance program split by ring share.
- Variable: heat-pump electricity actually served (`E x f`) at $0.245/kWh residential for the corridor and $0.108/kWh industrial for central equipment and pumping; pumping at 1.5 percent of delivered heat; backup fuel at propane cost divided by 0.9 boiler efficiency; heat purchase from the data center at $0 per MWh (the Deep Green precedent gives heat away).

### Levelized cost of heat

```
LCOH(r) = sum over rings [ sum_class( capex_class x CRF(r, life_class) ) + opex_fixed + opex_var ]
          ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
                                 sum over rings [ annual heat delivered to customers ]
```

Units: USD per MWh of heat delivered at the customer meter (not at the plant gate). Three discount rates are reported: 4 percent (`coop_4pct`), 7 percent (`utility_7pct`, the headline), 10 percent (`private_10pct`). Ring LCOH uses the same formula restricted to one ring at 7 percent (`lcoh_ring`). The code also computes a 30 percent investment-tax-credit variant (`lcoh_itc7`), published only as an incentive scenario because a waste-heat network probably does not qualify (`research/verification.md` 9d-i).

### Tariff rule

```
reference = incumbent propane at appliance efficiency
          = propane_usd_gal / (26.8 kWh/gal) / 0.85 x 1000     [USD/MWh]
tariff    = (1 - 0.20) x reference                            (0.8 x propane)
low-income = (1 - 0.35) x reference, applied to 20 percent of corridor customers
blended   = 0.8 x tariff + 0.2 x low-income
on-site tariff = $50/MWh (fixed)
```

The reference for Site 1 is Con Edison steam ($118.7/MWh). Revenue is on-site MWh x $50 plus corridor MWh x blended tariff.

### NPV and funding gap

```
NPV = ( revenue - opex - annualized_capex ) x annuity(7%, 30),    annuity = (1 - (1 + r)^-n) / r
funding_gap = max(0, -NPV)
```

Because capex enters as an equivalent annual cost by life class, replacement is included. The corridor stand-alone gap and the whole-project gap are both computed; the headline is the whole project because the on-site surplus cross-subsidizes the corridor (`report.py`, `extras.cba`). A DC-exit case (`dc_exit`, year 10) computes stranded data-center-specific capex (straight line) and the replacement-source cost to keep the corridor running.

## 7. Impact

`impact.py:evaluate`:

```
CO2_displaced = sum_ring( heat_served_MWh x fuel_share / appliance_efficiency x EF_fuel x 1000 )     [kg]
CO2_added     = ( HP_electricity + pumping ) x EF_grid x 1000  +  backup_fuel x EF_propane x 1000   [kg]
CO2_avoided   = (CO2_displaced - CO2_added) / 1000                                                   [t/yr]
```

Emission factors (kg CO2 per kWh, `config/impact.yaml`): grid 0.1101 (eGRID2023 NYUP 242.8 lb/MWh), marginal grid 0.315 (NYISO Zone C midpoint, reported as a sensitivity), liquid propane 0.2145 (62.87 kg/MMBtu), heating oil 0.2520, natural gas 0.1810. Fuel mixes displaced are assumptions: on-site 100 percent propane, corridor 45 percent propane, 15 percent oil, 35 percent electric, 5 percent other (from Census B25040 non-gas shares), town 40/30/30 propane/oil/gas. Electric resistance displaced is credited at the grid factor.

Energy reuse metrics (HDR deck p17-18):

```
ERF = reused_heat / IT_energy
ERE = ( PUE x IT_energy - reused_heat ) / IT_energy          PUE = 1.2 (assumption)
```

Other impact outputs: fan electricity avoided (2 percent of rejected heat that is reused), jobs (greenhouse jobs interpolated from RII Table 2 between 10 and 65 acres, plus aquaculture at 40 t per job, 12 recreation jobs, 8 network operations jobs), food (greenhouse yield 40 kg/m2 plus fish tonnage). No lake-water saving is claimed: closed-loop dry cooling is the water win and is the developer's own design.

## 8. Sensitivity and scenarios

`model.py:tornado` re-runs the full model with one driver at a time at low and high values (config `tornado:`) and reports LCOH at 7 percent. `model.py:scenarios` runs the capture-fraction cases (0.40, 0.75, 0.85), the 320 MW full build, the 65 deg C capture case for the town ring, central heat pump at the residential rate, and low propane. `model.py:breakeven_homes` scans corridor homes from 50 to 2,000 under three capex-payer assumptions. Results are in [results.md](results.md).

## 9. Offtaker scoring

`scoring.py:build` ranks candidate heat customers with a weighted score of temperature fit (0.20), size (0.25), proximity (0.25), displaced fuel (0.15) and community value (0.15), using haversine distance from the site. Weights, full-load hours and loads are assumptions in `config/offtakers.yaml`. Loads for modeled customers come from the demand model; others are floor area x intensity and are flagged as assumptions.
