# Evidence base: studies and operating projects

Status: DRAFT for review. Model numbers unchanged; this file only cites. Every URL below was opened (fetched) on 2026-10-04 unless marked [unverified]. [unverified] means the claim came only from a search-result snippet, or the page opened but did not contain the number.

Our-side values are read from `outputs/site2.json` and `research/model-notes.md` (model output, no URL).

## Summary

- Literature supports the architecture: data-center heat plus heat pumps into district heating is operating at scale (Odense 45 MW, Stockholm, Tallaght), with more being built (Espoo; Kajaani 30 MW, 12 MW scheduled from May 2026 and 18 MW in 2027), and low-temperature or ambient loops have measured seasonal COPs of 3 to 5.
- Our corridor building-heat-pump COP of 4.71 sits at the top of the measured 5th-generation range (3 to 5), so "consistent but upper end". The town-ring central COP of 6.0 is a clip at the cap, not a result, and is optimistic.
- Our weakest-supported inputs are the $16k per-home heat pump and the ~$449/m ambient pipe cost (no ambient-loop cost source opened; insulated 2-pipe DH in Ireland is EUR 782-869/m). Both are optimistic until sourced.
- The corridor ring has a peak heat density of about 0.33 kW per metre of pipe (6.84 MW over 20.9 km), versus a 1.2 kW/m feasibility threshold cited for traditional DH. The model already shows this: corridor LCOH $285.8/MWh. The $106 blended LCOH is pulled down by the $40.6 on-site ring.
- Not covered by any source here: propane price, the 0.8 x propane tariff rule, the $16k heat pump unit cost, and the exact Turner & Townsend $/W figures.

## Contradictions and caveats

1. **Corridor economics are the weak link, and the literature agrees.** Our corridor LCOH ($285.8/MWh) is above every cited 5GDHC case (EUR 101-186/MWh [unverified range], EUR 128 at Schallstadt) and above our own air-source heat pump comparator ($96.9). The headline $106 blended LCOH is a mix of a $40.6 on-site ring and this corridor. Quote both.
2. **Density threshold.** The corridor peak density (about 0.33 kW/m) is far below the 1.2 kW/m traditional-DH threshold; 9 of 16 surveyed 5GDHC systems were also below it, so we are not unique, but those systems have cooling revenue and we do not.
3. **COP.** 4.71 on the corridor is at the high end of measured SCOP (3 to 5); the survey figure includes pumping and field losses. The town ring uses COP 6.0 (a clip). A literature-based 60-70 C sink from 45 C is 4.3 to 5.5 in simulation, and the IEA reference is 3.2. We look optimistic here.
4. **Pipe cost.** $449/m is about half the cost of insulated 2-pipe DH in Ireland (EUR 782-869/m, 2020). The saving depends on uninsulated ambient pipe and low rural trenching cost; we have no opened source for either. Treat as optimistic until sourced.
5. **Heat pump unit cost** ($16k) has no opened source. The model notes name it as the binding cost.
6. **Odense numbers disagree between sources** (100,000 vs 215,000 MWh/yr; 7,000 vs 12,000 homes), likely project phasing; we cite the Ramboll page (45 MW) and keep the Alfa Laval page as the earlier figure. Do not quote 100,000 MWh without the date.
7. **Our capture share is small.** Delivered heat is 50.6 GWh, 6.5% of available heat. Our ERF is 0.0462, below the German EnEfG 10% requirement for new data centres; NY has no such rule. Precedents that make the project economic (Odense, Espoo, Stockholm) use large existing DH networks with heat sold at a price; Lansing has none, so the load must be built from scratch.
8. **Data-center side is not free money.** The DTU/EEM 2023 study finds recovery needs priority access and high heat prices to pay back, and notes few such systems exist. Our funding gap ($26.0M PV) is this problem, assigned to a Community Benefit Agreement.
9. **Precedent ownership is public or non-profit.** Tallaght (council-owned), Odense (utility-owned heat pump), and Danish co-ops (non-profit by law) all separate the heat utility from the data-center operator. Our co-op 4% finance case ($89.5/MWh) assumes a similar structure; we found no opened source for the 4% rate itself.
10. **Greenhouse**: our 310 kWh/m2 is in the Dutch range. Lansing is colder, so that is not obviously optimistic; the bigger unknown is crop and tariff ($50/MWh assumed).
11. **Not verified here:** propane price and tariff rule, the Framingham comparison, Saskatoon greenhouse number, the 4G/5G EUR ranges, and the exact T&T $/W figures.

## Three lines for judges

1. Data-center heat plus large heat pumps feeding district heating is already running at utility scale (Odense 45 MW since 2019, Tallaght, Stockholm), with more under construction (Espoo, Kajaani), and it is already required by law in Europe (EU EED Art. 26, German EnEfG 10 to 20% reuse).
2. Our cheap numbers come from putting the greenhouse and fish campus next to the data center ($40.6/MWh); the ambient-loop corridor to homes is $285.8/MWh and needs a community-benefit subsidy, which is what the literature on low-density networks and data-center heat sales also says.
3. Where we are most exposed: COP 4.71 (top of a 3 to 5 measured range), COP 6.0 on the town ring (a cap), and pipe and in-home heat pump costs that we could not tie to an opened source.
