# Risk matrix: Lansing NY waste-heat reuse (Site 2, "Lake Hawkeye")

Working site: former Cayuga Generating Station, Lansing NY (Lake Hawkeye / TeraWulf, base case ~150 MW phase 1 per [S2]). Three-ring concept per [S2]: Ring 1 on-site, Ring 2 corridor, Ring 3 town center (conditional).

## Scoring method

- **Likelihood (L) and Impact (I) are the team's own judgment, scored 1-5. They are NOT sourced facts.** Where a fact drives a score, it is cited with a source key from the Sources section. Every source key in that section carries "(verified 2026-10-03)".
- L: 1 = rare, 2 = unlikely, 3 = possible, 4 = likely, 5 = near certain over the project life.
- I: 1 = negligible, 2 = minor (revenue/uptime dip), 3 = moderate (one ring delayed or re-priced), 4 = major (a ring or the anchor lost), 5 = project-ending.
- Score = L x I (max 25). Bands: 15-25 high, 8-14 medium, 1-7 low.
- Residual = our judgment of L x I after the mitigation is in place. Residual scores are targets, not guarantees.
- Owner = the party that controls the lever, not the party that suffers. Parties: **TeraWulf** (data center operator), **HeatCo** (the heat network owner/operator; structure per `docs/ownership-deal.md`, which was a skeleton when this file was written), **Town** (Lansing Town Board), **NYSEG/PSC**, **Offtaker** (greenhouse/aquaculture/building owner), **State** (Governor, DEC, DPS), **Courts**, **Federal** (Congress/IRS).
- Key framing risk: R01, R02, R06 and R13 threaten the data center itself. Heat reuse has no supply without it. Our lever is making the conditions for "yes" concrete ([S2]), not controlling the vote.

## Heat-continuity cascade

What happens to delivered heat when the data center's heat output falls (outage, curtailment, tenant loss, or R03 slow ramp). No engineering outputs existed in the repo when this was written (`outputs/` and `config/` empty), so the table uses symbols. Numbers marked [unverified] are pending the 8,760-hour model. The only sourced numbers are the weather design point and the target.

Symbols (all MW or MWh of thermal energy, at the network): D(t) = heat demand; Q(t) = heat available from the data center after the heat pump; E = usable thermal storage (MWh_th); B = backup boiler capacity (MW_th); F = deficit = max(0, D - Q).

| step | what happens | covers | duration / formula | unmet heat if this step is the last one | numbers |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0. Normal | Q >= D. Heat pump lifts data-center heat to network temperature. | All demand. | Continuous. | 0 | Q and D [unverified] pending model. |
| 1. Heat falls | Q drops below D (outage, curtailment, ramp shortfall). Deficit F = D - Q appears. | Nothing extra yet. Controls shed lowest-priority loads first (Ring 3, then Ring 2 corridor, Ring 1 last; vulnerable customers last). | Instant. | F x time. | F [unverified]. |
| 2. Storage discharges | Thermal storage supplies F. | F up to the discharge rate. | Storage hours = E / F, so hours rise as F falls. A small curtailment (F small) lasts long. | After E is empty: F x (outage duration - E/F). | E [unverified]; storage size is a model output, not an assumption here. |
| 3. Backup boilers fire | Electric (or delivered-fuel) boilers cover F beyond storage. Piped gas is not available near the plant ([S1]). | min(F, B). | Until the data center recovers or the boiler energy limit is reached. | max(0, F - B) x time. | B [unverified]; sized so B >= design-day F (formula, see design-point row). |
| 4. Customers' own systems | Households and farms keep their existing propane/oil/gas systems during early years; the network is a supplement, not the only source (supply risk makes offtakers unwilling to rely on one source: [S6]). | Residual F - B. | Automatic via thermostat/hybrid controls. | Falls to 0 for dual-fuel customers; stays > 0 for any customer that removed its old system. | Share of dual-fuel customers [unverified]. |
| 5. Unmet heat | Any F not covered by steps 2-4 is unmet heat. | n/a | Unmet energy U = integral of max(0, D - Q - discharge - B) dt. | U > 0 | Target: 0 unmet hours with backup (PLAN.md section 0). |

Design-point stress case (compound): data-center heat lost on the coldest design hour. Weather: ASHRAE 99.6% winter design temperature 0.8F (-17.3C) at the Ithaca airport station ([S1]). Required: B + discharge rate >= D_design (formula). D_design [unverified]. Storage must also cover the time to restore B or the data center: E >= (D_design - B) x t_restore.

Ownership of each step: step 1 and 2 = HeatCo operations; step 3 = HeatCo; data-center restart = TeraWulf; step 4 = Offtaker. Metrics to publish: storage-hours delivered, backup-boiler hours, unmet hours (target 0), and heat-delivered share by ring.

Precedent for a backup pattern: the Exmouth pool heated by data-center heat reported the heat holds about 30C for 60% of the time and the pool's gas boiler covers the rest ([S11]). That is a small on-site case, not a reliability benchmark for a network.

## Cooling independence statement

**Data-center cooling never depends on any offtaker or on the heat network.** The heat network is a side-stream customer of the data center's waste heat. If every offtaker stops taking heat, or the network is shut off, the data center keeps cooling.

- The heat rejection path that always exists is TeraWulf's own: a sealed closed-loop liquid cooling system with dry (fan) coolers and no consumptive water in operation ([S1] cooling design rows; [S2]).
- The side-stream heat exchanger takes heat in parallel to that path. The default valve state is full flow to the dry coolers. Our heat exchanger fails safe by bypass; TeraWulf holds the right to isolate it at any time (proposed HSA term, see `docs/ownership-deal.md`). The organizer-site analysis also describes the design as "zero risk to DC dry coolers" ([S10]).
- Correction to a common assumption: the lake is NOT a fallback heat sink. The DEC 1.008 MGD withdrawal permit is restricted to site maintenance, sump pumping and dust control, not continuous cooling ([S9], [S1]). Proposing it as a cooling backup would be wrong and would feed R14. The HSA should say the permit is not used for cooling.
- Consequences: summer surplus (R15) is an economic loss, never a cooling emergency. Heat-network failures (R16, R25) cannot propagate into the data center beyond the isolation valves.
- Hard constraint source: PLAN.md section 0 ("Data center cooling never depends on offtakers").
- Dry-cooler capacity sufficient for 100% of heat rejection at summer design (ASHRAE 1.0% 85.7F dry bulb / 70.3F wet bulb, [S1]) is a TeraWulf design fact we have not seen: [unverified]. Ask TeraWulf to confirm in the HSA recitals.
