# Executive summary

Thermal Commons (Harsh Agarwal, Linson Lee, Aryaman Bhaskar, Philip Matchev). Site: Lake Hawkeye / TeraWulf, former Cayuga coal plant, Lansing, NY. Comparison only: 111 Eighth Avenue, New York City.

## The Deep Green lesson and our answer

Deep Green proposed a 24 MW heat-reuse data center in Lansing, **Michigan** — more than $120 million — to feed the Board of Water & Light downtown hot-water system and save about $1.1 million a year in gas. Announced 5 November 2025, the rezoning application was withdrawn on 6 April 2026 [1] (verified 2026-10-03). A long connection into someone else’s district system, offered as goodwill, did not hold.

Our answer for Lansing, **New York**, is the condition under which the town could say yes. The Thermal Commons co-op would own the community pipes and heat pumps. TeraWulf would sell only a sidestream of heat. A Heat Supply Agreement would run with the ground lease. A Community Benefit Agreement would fund the gap so prices stay under propane and oil. The first customers come to the heat on the industrial site. The town-center main is out of the base case.

On 29 September 2026 the Town Board directed its attorney to draft a data-center ban. It has not voted on the ban. Next year’s proposed budget sets aside $500,000 for legal costs [2] (verified 2026-10-03). 607 News Now reported 36 of 38 speakers opposed; that count is a single source [3] (verified 2026-10-03). HDR’s site pack states there are no designated disadvantaged communities nearby, so this is not an environmental-justice claim [4]. About 26% of occupied town homes heat with propane and about 8% with fuel oil [5] (verified 2026-10-03). NYSEG invoked a gas moratorium in the Town of Lansing in 2015; the July 2025 gas plan still listed a low-pressure problem. Whether the moratorium still binds in 2026 is [unverified] [6] (verified 2026-10-03). Town hall and the library are 5.7 miles from the plant; the school campus is 7.0 miles [7] (verified 2026-10-03).

## What the heat does

The model base is **150 MW** IT. The website’s phase-1 split does not appear in the latest filing. TeraWulf’s 5 August 2026 release states about **400 MW gross / 320 MW critical IT**, with operations not contemplated until about 2029 [8] (verified 2026-10-03). At load factor 0.8 and capture 0.75, **777.6 GWh/yr** is available at 50 °C (88.8 MW average). Phases 1–2 deliver **50,576 MWh/yr**, **6.5%** of that supply. A 320 MW build-out does not add customers [9].

On site: greenhouse, aquaculture, and a recreation center — **37,076 MWh/yr**, 45 °C, 0.5 km, **$40.6/MWh** at 7%. Corridor: **500 homes**, 13,500 MWh/yr, 20 °C ambient loop, 20.9 km, **$285.8/MWh**, only **0.65 MWh per metre per year**, so it waits on signed clusters. Town center: 3,577 MWh/yr, 65 °C, 14 km, **$734/MWh**, 1,840 MWh/yr of pipe loss, cost gate failed [9].

Cooling stays on the developer’s sealed dry-cooler loop, a developer claim of no lake draw or discharge [10] (verified 2026-10-03). Recovery is a sidestream. Backup is **0.73%** of annual heat; the peak share on counted peak hours is 4.7% [9]. DEC renewed a withdrawal of up to **1,008,000 gallons per day** on 13 April 2026 for Cayuga Operating Company LLC, limited to maintenance, sump pumping, and dust control [11] (verified 2026-10-03). Heat reuse does not save that water. The covenant is to keep the permit off cooling.

## The price and the ask

Phases 1–2 capital is **$38.8 million**; many unit costs are ASSUMPTIONS inside the model. Blended cost is **$89.5/MWh** at a 4% co-op rate ($106.1 at 7%, $124.6 at 10%). The tariff is **$108.9/MWh**, 20% under a propane equivalent of $136.1/MWh: **$735/yr** on a 27 MWh home, and **$1,266/yr** versus oil at $155.8/MWh. Gas at $64.2/MWh is cheaper, so connected gas customers are not the market [9]. Holding that tariff leaves a **$26.0 million** present-value gap, **1.7%** of an assumed $1,500 million campus. The $10/W campus cost is the midpoint of Turner & Townsend’s 2025 band of $6.6–13.3/W and is an ASSUMPTION, not a TeraWulf budget [9][12]. The ground lease is 183 acres for 80 years [13] (verified 2026-10-03). The heat contract opens at 10 years, with renewals and co-op step-in if the data center leaves. If it exits in year 10, the model shows **$5.7 million** stranded and a **$10.4 million** replacement source [9]. New York authority to charter a heat co-op is [unverified] [14]. Federal credits are out of the base case [15] (verified 2026-10-03).

Avoided carbon to publish is **11,408 t/yr**. The model file’s 11,456 overstates that result [16]. Energy reuse is **4.6%** of IT energy because demand, not capture, is the limit [9]. Cayuga Lake is phosphorus-impaired in the site pack [4]. Closed-loop growing is the nutrients response. No nutrient mass and no lake-water gallons are claimed.
