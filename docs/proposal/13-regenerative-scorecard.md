## 5. What this proposal is (and the covenant frame)

The Lansing Town Board action on 2026-09-29 is the political constraint. The Thermal Commons co-op is framed as a binding heat-reuse and community-benefit covenant that could make a project acceptable, not as a sustainability add-on. Detail is filled from organizer text and verification notes.

## 6. Gaps and non-claims

- Do not claim heat reuse saves Cayuga Lake water 1:1. Cooling is described as sealed closed-loop water/propylene-glycol rejected by fan coolers (dry coolers), with no consumptive water during operation. A DEC withdrawal permit is a separate fact.
- Do not mix Deep Green / Board of Water & Light (Lansing, Michigan) with Lansing, New York. Michigan is precedent only.
- Do not treat ~400 MW as the current proposal. Base is ~150 MW phase 1; 300–400 MW is a build-out scenario.
- Do not overclaim equity. Site pack: no designated disadvantaged communities nearby.
- Town-center anchors (school campus, town hall, library) are roughly 5–7 miles from the plant. Priority users come to the heat on unused acreage. A town-center main is only in scope if its economics pass.
