## 0. Frame: what the stakeholder map is telling us

- The Town Board on 2026-09-29 directed its attorney to draft a local law banning data centers. The law is not yet introduced and has no deadline. IV-BAN, FL1-OCT2 (verified 2026-10-03).
- Heat reuse is therefore not a green add-on. It is, at best, one condition among several under which a skeptical town could say yes. PLAN "Reality check" (verified 2026-10-03).
- Neither the Cayuga Lake Watershed Network page nor the Sustainable Finger Lakes page mentions heat reuse (fetched 2026-10-03). CLWN, SFLX. Inference: the opposition has not yet been offered heat reuse as a trade, so we do not know whether they would treat it as a concession or as greenwashing. Test before we pitch.
- DATA HEAT states that local benefits matter more as siting opposition grows. `resources/text/DATA_HEAT_-_Sector_Couling_..._3MAR26.txt`, Appendix A p. 35, conclusion (verified 2026-10-03). It also says heat reuse interests differ by party: the data center cares about time to market, while a heat user values the heat more in relative terms. Same file, PDF p. 19 (verified 2026-10-03).

## 3. Stakeholder profiles

For each: wants, fears, what they get from our proposal. "PROPOSAL" lines are our claims. Where a number is used, it is either sourced or marked "derived" or "assumption".

Reference prices used below (carried from `research/facts-site2.md` section 4, whose underlying NYSERDA/EIA pages this lane did not re-fetch, so treat as [unverified by this lane]): propane $2.849/gal; fuel oil $5.186/gal; delivered heat about $0.125 and $0.156 per kWh_th; NYSEG electric $0.245/kWh; heat pump electricity-only cost $0.054 to $0.070 per kWh_th at assumed COP 4.5 to 3.5 (assumption in FS2). ### 3.1 Residents

- **Evidence:** Overwhelming opposition at the 2026-09-29 meeting; concerns were water quality, noise, electricity rates, light, construction, climate, and whether the jobs last. FL1-OCT2 (verified 2026-10-03). Petition "Reject the Development of an AI Data Center on Cayuga Lake" reported at about 20,938 signatures as of June 22. ITHACA-PETITION search summary (verified 2026-10-03). Earlier reported at about 17,000. FL1-JUN18.
- **Wants:** no noise or light, protected lake, stable electric bills, believable operator. FL1-OCT2, SFLX.
- **Fears:** rising electric bills; a developer they distrust. IV-BAN lists distrust of TeraWulf (verified 2026-10-03).
- **Segment with real self-interest in heat:** rural homes off the gas system. Derived from FS2: propane plus fuel oil about 34% of about 4,450 households is roughly 1,500 households (derived, assumption-level).
- **PROPOSAL gives:** an option (not an obligation) to buy heat at a tariff pegged below propane/oil cost. Illustrative operating-cost-only comparison from FS2 inputs: $0.054 to $0.070 per kWh_th versus $0.125 (propane) and $0.156 (oil). This excludes network capex and margin, so the real tariff must come from `outputs/` model results [pending]. It is not a reason to approve the data center; it is a way to make the bargain visible to the households with the least alternatives.
- **Engagement risk:** a household savings promise that is later undercut by a distance-driven capex penalty (PLAN: town-center anchors about 5 to 8 miles away) would confirm distrust. Quote ranges and conditions, never a single headline saving.

### 3.2 Town of Lansing board

- **Members (as reported):** Supervisor Ruth Groff (supports a total ban), Deputy Supervisor Joe Wetmore (leaning toward prohibition), Laurie/Lori Hemmings (interested in a total ban), Judy Drake (earlier concerned about economic development, increasingly favoring a ban), Christine Montague (reported undecided, per IV-BAN; FL1-OCT2 does not list her). IV-BAN, FL1-OCT2 (verified 2026-10-03). Name spelling differs between sources.
- **Process still ahead:** attorney drafts; introduction at a public meeting; public hearing; Planning Board review; Tompkins County planning review; final Board vote. Attorney said drafting will take time. FL1-OCT2 (verified 2026-10-03). Attorney named as Megan Doherty in FL1-OCT2.
- **Prior action:** the ZBA ruled the data center fits "general processing" on 2025-12-22 (3-2). IV-ZBA, ITHACA-ZBA (verified 2026-10-03). Board withdrew a construction-pause ordinance in Dec 2025. CS-DEC2025 (title only; details [unverified]).
- **Budget:** about $500,000 set aside in next year's budget for legal costs. IV-BAN (verified 2026-10-03).
- **Wants:** defensible law, no litigation loss, tax base and brownfield reuse if the project were safe, a possible host-benefit agreement as an alternative to a ban. IV-BAN (verified 2026-10-03).
- **Fears:** litigation cost; enforcement burden; backlash from a hostile room. IV-BAN, FL1-OCT2.
- **PROPOSAL gives:** (a) a legal and political alternative frame, "conditions under which Lansing could say yes", expressed as a binding Community Benefit Agreement plus Heat Supply Agreement with reversion rights; (b) a measurable local benefit that the town can verify and enforce; (c) the town's own capital plan shows Town Hall heating by a 220,000 BTU gas condensing boiler (OFF, citing the Town capital plan PDF, verified 2026-10-03), so the Town itself is a modest heat customer. Do not oversell (c); distance to Town Hall is the open issue in section 2.
- **Caution:** NY Open Meetings Law limits private serial briefings of a quorum. Confirm approach with counsel before one-on-one board contacts [unverified, legal check needed].

### 3.3 Tompkins County and Seneca County

- **Tompkins County Legislature:** on 2026-01-20 voted 14-1 with 1 recusal (Dan Wakeman, a TeraWulf employee) asking DEC to reject the modified permit and require a new application and review. Mike Sigler cast the sole no, arguing the permit cannot be used for data center cooling and that the resolution could open a door. ITHACA-TOMP, IV-TOMP-SEARCH (verified 2026-10-03). Legislator Irene Weiser noted the permit lacks basic safeguards such as meters. ITHACA-TOMP (verified 2026-10-03).
- **Seneca County Board:** unanimous 2026-03-10 resolution asking DEC to reject the modified permit and to run a new environmental review. FLTIMES-SENECA, SENECA-MINUTES (verified 2026-10-03). Seneca is across the lake; it is not the host and has no zoning role. Its interest is lake water quality.
- **Wants:** lake protection, metering and monitoring, a state review; some members want tax revenue (Sigler). ITHACA-TOMP, FL1-OCT2.
- **Fears:** permit used for something other than its stated purposes; ecological harm to Cayuga Lake. ITHACA-TOMP.
- **PROPOSAL gives:** (a) a verifiable, monitored heat export that does not add lake withdrawals; (b) a covenant that the 1.008 MGD permit stays out of data center cooling, which addresses the exact scenario the county raised (PLAN concept; legal form to be confirmed [unverified]); (c) a county planning-review file (GML 239 step in the ban law process, FL1-OCT2) that shows measurable local benefits. County has a role as reviewer in the ban process.

### 3.4 Lansing Central School District

- **Facts:** three schools, 371 + 355 + 392 students (high school, middle, elementary). NCES CCD 2024-25 via OFF (verified 2026-10-03). The district faced a $2.2M deficit; three board members resigned on March 23, 2026. IV-SCHOOLBOARD search summary (verified 2026-10-03). TeraWulf donated $10,000 to the district in 2025 and projects that much of its tax revenue would go to the district. IV-PLANNING search summary (verified 2026-10-03). No district statement on the project was found. FL1-OCT2 (verified 2026-10-03).
- **History with heat:** the Lansing NPA funded building efficiency work and higher-efficiency gas boiler upgrades at the high and middle schools. NPA-CCE (verified 2026-10-03). So the district's boilers are relatively recent investments; displacement economics are weaker than for propane homes. Inference.
- **Wants:** stable budget under the tax cap; predictable tax revenue. IV-SCHOOLBOARD.
- **Fears:** instability; being used as a prop in a political fight; capital cost of retrofits. Inference.
- **PROPOSAL gives:** an optional heat connection after Phase 3 gates are met (PLAN three-ring concept), with no capital cost to the district if structured as a heat service contract [PROPOSAL, no numbers]. Campus is 7.0 mi per FS2 or about 6.5 mi per OFF-derived km; both long. Do not promise early connection. The honest message is "last ring, conditional".

### 3.5 NYSEG

- **Facts:** gas moratorium on new service in the Lansing area, caused by pressure limits, in place since about 2015. ITHACA-RECLAIM, NPA-CCE (verified 2026-10-03). Instead of a new pipeline, NYSEG funded a Non-Pipes Alternative portfolio: a search summary of the Public Service Commission NPA report gives five projects, $9.0 million total, and a targeted demand cut of about 49.3 MCFH. NPA-DPS (verified 2026-10-03). The program is reported as reaching 47% of goal by year three, a dated figure that is probably out of date [unverified current status]. Community campaign gives stackable heat pump incentives by zone: 100% in Zone 1, 40% in Zone 2, 10% in Zone 3. NPA-CCE (verified 2026-10-03).
- **TeraWulf link:** power from NYSEG grid; TeraWulf expects to invest about $15 million in local electrical upgrades for the first 138 MW. SEC-8K (verified 2026-10-03).
- **Wants:** a reliable gas system without a pipeline; recover costs; avoid stranded assets. Inference from NPA history.
- **Fears:** a rival heat provider reducing gas and electric throughput; heat pump load adding winter peaks on an upstate system. Inference.
- **PROPOSAL gives:** (a) more gas demand reduction in the constrained area without utility capex (the NPA goal); (b) a possible role as network operator or off-taker, since NY's thermal energy network law is utility-oriented (UTENJA, FS2 section 6, [source generic, unverified by this lane]); (c) a large-load customer that pays its own upgrades.
- **Engagement ask:** whether NPA zone incentives could stack with a heat network connection.

### 3.6 NYS DEC

- **Facts:** renewed the Cayuga Operating Company withdrawal permit on 2026-04-13 for about 1,008,000 gal/day to 2031-04-30; allowed uses are maintenance, sump pumping, dust control; non-transferable to TeraWulf. CS-DEC (verified 2026-10-03). Permit allows return water at up to 98.1 degrees F per a Finger Lakes Times summary, [exact source unverified]. EO 62 pauses discretionary DEC data center permits at 50 MW and up until DPS completes a GEIS. DLA-EO62 (verified 2026-10-03).
- **Wants:** a clean record on lake water; no surprise reclassification of a legacy permit. Inference.
- **Fears:** the permit being used for cooling without review; litigation. CS-DEC.
- **PROPOSAL gives:** a zero-added-withdrawal heat extraction on a sealed glycol loop, plus a voluntary monitoring regime for any non-cooling use of the permit [PROPOSAL, DEC willingness unverified]. Note a heat-recovery sidestream on the existing sealed loop should not need a new water permit, but confirm with DEC before claiming it [unverified].

### 3.7 NYS PSC and DPS

- **Facts:** Governor announced a PSC proceeding on large-load interconnection and cost allocation ("Energize NY Development"), so data centers pay their share. GOV-ENERGIZE (verified 2026-10-03). Under EO 62 DPS runs the GEIS covering energy demand, water, air, noise and disproportionate impacts on disadvantaged communities. DLA-EO62 (verified 2026-10-03). NYSEG's thermal network pilot work is under PSC Case 22-M-0429 per FS2 [unverified by this lane].
- **Wants:** ratepayers protected; thermal networks done by regulated utilities with tariffs; clean-heat targets.
- **Fears:** cost shifts to residential customers; unregulated heat sellers with no consumer protection. Inference.
- **PROPOSAL gives:** a concrete case study for the GEIS and the Community Investment Framework, where heat supply is a quantified community benefit, and a tariff design that is regulated or contract-backed.
- **Note:** the hackathon site pack states there are no disadvantaged communities nearby, so equity here is rural energy burden, not DAC status. FS2 section 2, citing `resources/text/2026_10_01_Hackathon_NYU_-_Suburban_Site_-_Lake_Hawkeye.txt` p. 24 [that file is in FS2 citation, not re-opened in this lane].

### 3.8 NYSERDA

Runs the Large-Scale Thermal Program (PON 5614), where thermal energy networks are eligible in a multiple-building category. NYSERDA-PON (verified 2026-10-03). Data Center Dynamics reports grants of up to $2 million for data center heat reuse under its heat recovery program (page returned 403; relied on search summary, [terms unverified]). DCD (verified 2026-10-03).

- **Fears:** a stranded project that fails to launch because of local opposition.
- **PROPOSAL gives:** a candidate pilot with explicit community governance, which is what the program designs reward. Quantify potential cost-share only after reading the current PON terms [pending].

### 3.9 TeraWulf

- **Facts:** 80-year lease on 183 acres of a 400-acre property; up to 400 MW right; first 138 MW targeted for service in 2026. SEC-8K, TOMPKINSWEEKLY-TERAWULF (verified 2026-10-03). VP Pace Ralli asked the board to keep the process open and evaluate on facts. IV-BAN, FL1-OCT2 (verified 2026-10-03). TeraWulf has said it will not seek a tax abatement, and has pledged $100,000 per year to a community fund. IV-PLANNING (verified 2026-10-03). Board asked whether the money would be a host benefit agreement or PILOT; the company representative was unsure. IV-PLANNING (verified 2026-10-03). FS2 says no public heat reuse plan has been announced by TeraWulf. FS2 section 1 [source generic, unverified by this lane].
- **Wants:** a permit path, time to power, a social license, no performance risk to the compute load. DATA HEAT says data centers weigh time to market and tenant needs over heat revenue. DATA HEAT PDF p. 19 (verified 2026-10-03).
- **Fears:** any heat obligation that threatens uptime or schedule; stranded capex; a precedent. Inference.
- **PROPOSAL gives (hard constraint from PLAN):** cooling never depends on offtakers; heat recovery is a side stream only; a third-party operator takes the heat-side capex; TeraWulf gets a credible community benefit it can show the board. Quantified value to TeraWulf: none sourced [pending model].
- **Ask of TeraWulf:** a term sheet for heat access at the glycol loop, a covenant not to use the 1.008 MGD permit for cooling, and acceptance of third-party metering.
