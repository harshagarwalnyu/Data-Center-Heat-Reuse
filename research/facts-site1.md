## Key implications for heat-reuse feasibility at Site 1

| Factor | Assessment |
|---|---|
| Heat source temperature | **Low**: Legacy air-cooled facility produces condenser water at ~30–35°C. No liquid cooling. Heat pump COP would be lower than a DLC-equipped facility producing 45–60°C water |
| Heat source quantity | **Moderate**: Estimated 30–40 MW IT load, but capture fraction from air-cooled systems is lower (~40–60% vs 70–80% for DLC). Effective capturable heat ~15–25 MW_th |
| Ownership complexity | **High**: Google owns the building but many tenants operate independent cooling plants. No single point of heat capture — would require cooperation from multiple parties |
| Offtaker density | **Very high**: Chelsea is dense urban environment. NYCHA housing (4,500+ residents), Chelsea Market, Hudson Yards all within 0.5 mi. Very high linear heat density |
| Competitor: ConEd steam | **High**: ConEd already serves Manhattan with district steam. More importantly, ConEd is already building the exact project (Chelsea UTEN pilot) that a heat-reuse proposal would describe |
| Regulatory tailwind | **Strong**: LL97 penalties, NYSERDA grants, UTENJA framework, data center moratorium all push toward heat reuse |
| Implementation barrier | **Very high**: Retrofitting a 1932 building with heat recovery in a multi-tenant environment, competing with an existing ConEd pilot, in the most expensive construction market in the US |
